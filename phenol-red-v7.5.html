How to read the results

CV: width consistency. Guide RMS / P95: deviation from the fitted parallel guides. Lower values indicate less deviation under matched imaging conditions; they are not pass/fail grades. Green edges must be correct before interpreting any measurement.

Center-constrained detection: a low-threshold whole-body redness profile first estimates the stripe axis independently of the dark-rim signal and final high-level edges. Left and right threshold crossings must lie on opposite sides of this initial axis; each side uses its own local peak so unequal rim darkness does not force both crossings onto one rim. Failed rows are re-tested on the original cross-section without vertical averaging, using the same boundary level and opposite-side constraint. Confirmed edges are included in width and roughness measurements and tagged as re-detected in the profile export. No interpolated coordinates enter the measurements. Measured edge arrays are never filled. The overlay connects consecutive confirmed edges. Unsupported gaps stay visible. If the line shifts across the initial axis or an edge cannot be confirmed, that row is marked uncertain. The yellow dashed center is estimated from the original whole red body, separately for each stripe, and is not the midpoint of the fitted blue guides. Green edges must straddle this yellow axis. Accepted green edges must also straddle the midpoint of the fitted blue guides as a second check. Points that fail this second guard are rejected and the guides refitted. Blue parallel guides are fitted to the accepted measurements, so this is a two-stage estimate, not an independent ground-truth reference. High settings and interpolation can change measured widths and roughness; inspect contours and use matched settings.

Parallel references: each measured stripe receives its own two straight, mutually parallel reference lines. A common slope and separate left/right offsets minimize summed horizontal squared deviations on valid rows. Their spacing is estimated from that stripe; there is no preset width and no shared width between 1 µL and 2 µL. The dashed blue overlay is an idealized reference, not a replacement for detected edges. Parallel-reference RMS and P95 use perpendicular distances to these lines. Missing rows are excluded from fitting and deviation statistics. There is no automatic pass/fail threshold. The reference does not validate an incorrectly detected boundary.

Boundary signal: Dark-red contrast uses a logarithmic red/green channel ratio with a small offset, followed by local background subtraction. It emphasizes relative green absorption rather than absolute red brightness. It is an image contrast measure, not calibrated optical density. Legacy redness uses red minus the mean of green and blue. The boundary level is a fraction between local background and a robust signal peak. A percentage does not define a physical material boundary; verify the contour against the original image. Signals and thresholds are not interchangeable: use the same method, level, imaging scale and lighting for comparisons.

Quality target: each stripe should have straight, parallel dark-red outer boundaries and constant width. The 1 µL and 2 µL stripes are evaluated separately; their widths need not match. Edge straightness RMS fits each edge with one straight line and combines the perpendicular residual RMS values. This removes overall tilt while retaining bending. Centerline bending RMS measures wandering of the midpoint. Overall edge angle difference measures taper only; width CV also detects local loss of parallelism. A zero angle difference alone does not imply good quality. Compare equal analysis lengths and calibrated resolution. Lower values are not trustworthy where detection coverage is poor.

Mean width = mean separation of the detected dark-red boundaries. With tilt correction enabled and accepted, cross-sections are perpendicular to one estimated sample direction. One rigid rotation is used for both stripes; individual rows are not straightened. Width is still an optical estimate, not a physical edge standard. Width CV (%) = 100 × standard deviation of width / mean width. Edge Rq is RMS edge deviation from a centered moving-average trend (optical contour roughness, not contact profilometry). Relative edge Rq (%) = 100 × edge Rq / mean width.

Undetected (%) = 100 × undetected rows / expected analysis rows; longest gap (%) = 100 × longest consecutive undetected sequence / expected analysis rows. Defects per 100 px = counted non-gap width-anomaly intervals / analyzed pixel length × 100. With sample length entered in millimeters, defects per 10 mm is additionally shown, normalized to the physical section length.

Different line lengths ON: the Start/End percentage is applied to each sample's own ROI height. OFF: the entire drawn ROI is analyzed. Results are calculated independently in both modes. A checkbox does not make edge Rq numerically invariant to different imaging resolution, noise, or observation length. For QC, use a common image magnification and identical settings, or a fixed calibrated segment length. Inspect overlays, especially where gaps or faint lines occur.

Automatic sample discovery assumes two approximately vertical pink/red stripes per sample. For rotation, perspective distortion, weak colors, unusual backgrounds or heavy JPEG artifacts, manually draw a tight ROI, adjust contrast threshold, and verify contours. When printed frames are present, tolerant discovery requires long neutral vertical sides and horizontal closure to locate sample boxes independently of dye intensity. Remaining unframed regions use red-signal discovery. Frame detection assumes approximately axis-aligned rectangular panels. Tolerant sample discovery uses lower projection and continuity thresholds, narrower peak spacing, and less required simultaneous stripe support; Strict restores the previous filters. Switching discovery mode does not change boundary measurements. Review candidate boxes because tolerant mode may include extra red marks. Automatic sample bounds require sustained evidence of both stripes; isolated spots and short label fragments are excluded. Detected dark horizontal frame borders constrain the range. The entire interval between accepted endpoints is retained, including internal gaps. These heuristics can still fail on unfamiliar layouts: inspect or redraw boxes before measuring. Tilt is estimated robustly from red-signal centroids over the middle 76% of each sample. Weak, inconsistent, or >25° estimates are left uncorrected and labelled uncertain. Crop tightly around both full stripes, excluding unrelated red marks. Perspective, lens distortion and out-of-plane deformation are not corrected. Bilinear resampling can affect pixel-scale roughness; use consistent resolution and settings. This is a prototype, not a validated QC standard. Images are processed entirely inside the browser.
